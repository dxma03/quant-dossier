## Core intuition

A market maker earns the spread **only when the quote is fairly priced**. If counterparties are better informed, fills become selectively toxic:

- buys arrive right before price moves up,
- sells arrive right before price moves down.

This is adverse selection: the maker is chosen *because* they are wrong.

## Practical implications

1. Wider spreads around information events.
2. Smaller size at top-of-book.
3. Faster quote refresh / cancellation.
4. Inventory limits that tighten under elevated toxicity.

## A simple decomposition

Expected PnL per fill can be sketched as:

\[
\mathbb{E}[\text{PnL}] \approx \text{Spread Capture} - \text{Adverse Selection Cost} - \text{Inventory Cost}.
\]

If the middle term dominates, quoting aggressively is irrational even with high turnover.

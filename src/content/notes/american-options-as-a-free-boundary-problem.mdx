For European options, exercise timing is fixed. For American options, the holder can exercise early, creating an optimal stopping problem.

## Variational inequality view

For an American put (under standard assumptions), value \(V(S,t)\) satisfies:

\[
\max\left(\mathcal{L}V,\ K-S - V\right)=0,
\]

where \(\mathcal{L}\) is the Black–Scholes operator. The inequality couples two regions:

1. **Continuation region**: hold option, PDE equality active.
2. **Exercise region**: intrinsic value binds.

The unknown interface between these regions is the **free boundary**.

## Why this matters in practice

- Numerical schemes must track boundary location.
- Boundary movement drives early-exercise premium.
- Coarse time-stepping can misprice near expiry and deep ITM states.
